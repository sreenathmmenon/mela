# Pen Fight play experience — 7 September 2026

## Why this revision exists

Sreenath rejected the impact-only release because it did not visibly improve the game. This revision addresses the play screen, shot choices, camera, spectators and the result together. Success means an easier, more expressive match that uses the device well. Neither code changes nor this research establish a worldwide ranking or predict virality.

## Implemented design

- **Space:** the match shell spans the browser. The old 620px arena cap is removed for Pen Fight. Desktop uses a broad stage plus a control rail; phones compose the desk and controls vertically. Landscape phones retain a side rail. The authoritative 1000×1000 desk boundary stays the same; the viewport is no longer constrained to a square panel.
- **Desk:** five individually toned wood boards, fine grain, deterministic scratches and corner screw marks, more restrained lighting and a satin surface. Actual Three.js pen meshes, shadows and desk depth remain. A lower camera is fitted to wide displays.
- **View:** 3D desk and overhead cameras both use the same camera for drawing and input ray projection. Changing the camera does not move the pens or spend a turn. Camera changes are disabled during a drag or animated shot. Overhead hides duplicate floating name labels to leave the board clear.
- **Aim:** tap a point on the desk to set direction without spending a turn. Pull back from the body/cap/tip of your own pen and release to shoot. Cap/middle/tip target presets and keyboard controls remain.
- **Shot craft:** optional cut-left/straight/cut-right proposals send the existing legal `contact` values 20/50/80. The normal reducer validates and resolves these. A one-degree fine-aim slider exposes the full direction range. This is the existing deflection rule, not a new angular-momentum simulation or a guaranteed trajectory prediction.
- **Match:** compact named seats, actual round scores, two earned-round markers and a clear active-turn marker. The first screen is reserved for the current match; historical rivalry remains in the completed result.
- **Crowd:** powers now occupy the rail beside the desktop stage and follow the desk on mobile, ahead of the QR. Pending effects remain spectator-only. Dismissible feedback reports accepted/rejected actions without inserting blank space into the page.
- **Finish:** a large final score, real remembered moment, next-match action, existing link sharing, and a downloadable 1080×1350 PNG match card. The card uses actual participant names, score and crowd count. Shared text now names the actual opponent in human/agent matches.
- **Replay:** the last received completed-match motion can be replayed locally. This does not call a reducer or add history. Replay is session-local, not durable video storage. Reopening an old result still offers its saved card, but not a fabricated motion replay.
- **Return:** rematches scroll to the fresh desk and reset shot craft to straight.

## Verification evidence

- 130 deterministic tests pass, including the extended camera round-trip checks for both views and named-opponent sharing. Typecheck, production frontend build and transport build pass.
- Real SDK regression completes human-human and human-agent Pen Fight, rejects stolen seats/roles and duplicate/stale turns, verifies disconnect/reconnect ownership and named durable memories.
- Independent local player and phone spectator: spectator NUDGE + human cut shot from overhead + automatic MelaBot response converge on the same committed motion and positions (human 800,920 / rival 857,478); no page errors or horizontal overflow.
- Actual mouse drag shots work through both 3D and overhead camera projections. A tap at projected world coordinate 600,250 changes aim to precisely 600,250 without changing the shot sequence.
- A full browser match completes 2–1. Replay retains its final score and committed sequence. The downloaded PNG was opened and visually checked.
- Layout checked at 320×740, 390×844, 768×1024, 1440×950, 1920×1080 and 844×390 without horizontal overflow. Final first-screen flick bottoms measured 733px at 320×740 and 814px at 390×844. Desktop arena measures 1045×585 at 1440×950 and 1454×715 at 1920×1080.
